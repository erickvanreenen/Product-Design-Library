# Scan the competition

Studies how others solve the same job to find table-stakes patterns, gaps and better ideas, judged on experience quality rather than feature lists.

## Use when
- Before framing the problem or designing flows in an established space.

## Steps
1. Pick five to eight products: direct competitors (same problem, same audience), indirect ones (same problem, different audience) and one or two best-in-class examples from other fields.
2. Choose the key tasks to compare, taken from the needs or the brief.
3. Walk each task end to end in each product. Use real product flow libraries (for example Mobbin or Page Flows) where you cannot sign up (S21).
4. Record per task: supported or not, number of steps, experience quality (1 to 5), notable approaches, accessibility issues seen.
5. Note how each product names things. Conflicting vocabulary feeds `domain-glossary`.
6. Summarise: table stakes (everyone does it, users expect it), weaknesses everyone shares (opportunity), and distinctive approaches worth learning from.

## Guardrails
- Compare whole journeys, not single screens.
- Do not copy. Record the principle behind a pattern, not its pixels.
- Date the scan; competitors change.

## Output
A comparison matrix by task, short profiles, table stakes, gaps and references with links.

## Done when
- Each gap or table-stakes claim points to specific products in the matrix.
