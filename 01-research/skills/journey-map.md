# Map the journey

Shows an end-to-end experience for one persona and scenario, with emotions, pain points and the moments that make or break it. Scales up to an experience map or service blueprint when needed.

## Use when
- One persona, one path: journey map.
- The experience crosses products or channels: widen to an experience map.
- Staff and back-office work shape the experience: add service blueprint lanes.

## Steps
1. Set scope: persona, scenario, start and end points, current state or future state. Map the current state first.
2. Define five to seven stages from the user's point of view.
3. For each stage, record: goal, actions, touchpoints and channels, thoughts and questions, emotion (rated positive to negative), pain points and opportunities.
4. Draw the emotional curve and mark the moments of truth.
5. For an experience map, add how touchpoints connect, data passing between channels, and hand-offs between people and automation.
6. For a service blueprint, add lanes beneath the user's actions: what staff or systems do that users see (frontstage), what they do out of sight (backstage) and the support processes behind them. Mark the line of visibility.
7. Rank the top three to five opportunities by impact and feasibility.

## Guardrails
- Every pain point should cite a finding. Mark assumed ones.
- Include physical and offline touchpoints where they exist.

## Output
A stage table (stages across, the layers above down), the emotional curve, moments of truth and ranked opportunities.

## Done when
- The ranked opportunities trace to evidenced pain points.
