# Build inclusive personas

Creates two to four research-based personas built on behaviour, with disability, situational limits and context of use woven in from the start.

## Use when
- Decisions need a consistent picture of users, and research supports distinct behaviour patterns.

## Not for
- Replacing research. A persona built on assumptions must say so on its first line.

## Steps
1. Cluster behaviour patterns from the findings: motivations, approaches, confidence, context. Personas come from behaviour, not demographics.
2. Define two to four personas. For each: a name, a one-line quote from research, context, goals (functional, emotional, social), frustrations, current behaviour, a short scenario and design implications.
3. Weave in ability. Write disability into the person, with their assistive technology and how it shapes good and bad experiences, never as a separate "accessibility persona".
4. Add context of use to every persona: environment, devices, energy level, who else is present, and the situational limits that come with it (glare, noise, one hand busy, time pressure, second language, stress).
5. Apply the minimum mix for four or more personas: at least one with a permanent disability, one in a situational limit, two differing in technical confidence, and variety in age, language and context.
6. For the core tasks, map how each works across ability ranges (vision, hearing, motor, cognitive): works well, works with friction, needs a workaround, or fails. Mark where it starts to fail.
7. Name the primary persona the design must satisfy first, with the reason.
8. List the research gaps behind each persona.

## Guardrails
- No stock-photo stereotypes, invented quotes or demographic padding that does not change a decision.
- Label assumption-based personas clearly as provisional.

## Output
Persona cards, an ability-range table for core tasks, the primary persona and gaps.

## Done when
- Each persona traces to findings, includes context of use, and the set meets the minimum mix.
