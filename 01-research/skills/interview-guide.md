# Write an interview guide

Produces a non-leading interview guide that gets people to describe real past experiences, so you learn what they do and why, not what they think they might do.

## Use when
- The research plan calls for interviews or contextual inquiry.

## Not for
- Usability sessions with tasks, use `usability-test-plan` (07).

## Steps
1. Confirm the priority questions, the participants and the length (usually 45 to 60 minutes).
2. Structure the session:
   - **Introduction** (2 to 3 min): purpose, consent, recording, "there are no wrong answers".
   - **Warm-up** (3 to 5 min): role, context, a typical day.
   - **Core** (20 to 30 min): one block per research question. Anchor in a real event: "Tell me about the last time you..." Then walk the timeline: what triggered it, what they tried, what they hoped for, what worried them, what happened next.
   - **Specific scenarios** (10 to 15 min): walk through a real task or artefact they have.
   - **Wrap-up** (3 to 5 min): "What should I have asked?", thanks, next steps.
3. Write probes for every core question: "Tell me more", "Why did that matter?", "What did you do then?", "Can you show me?"
4. Ask about emotional and social sides as well as practical ones: how they felt, who else was involved, how it looked to others.
5. Look for workarounds: spreadsheets, notes, emails to self. A workaround marks a real need.
6. Test every question. Rewrite any that leads ("Was that frustrating?" becomes "How did that feel?"), assumes ("Would you use..."), or allows a plain yes or no.
7. Add facilitator notes: stay neutral, let silences run, park tangents, watch the time.

## Guardrails
- No hypotheticals ("Would you...?"). Ask about what happened.
- No selling or demoing during discovery interviews.
- Adapt for access needs: offer captions, extra time, a screen-reader-friendly format, or text-based sessions.

## Output
A ready-to-use guide with timings, questions, probes and facilitator notes.

## Done when
- Every question maps to a research question, and none can be answered with yes or no.
