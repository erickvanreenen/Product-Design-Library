# Visual perception principles

How people group and read what they see, and how to use it.

| Principle | What it says | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity | Things close together look related, more strongly than any other cue. | Use less space within a group than between groups. |
| Similarity | Things that look alike (colour, shape, size) seem to belong together. | Give one kind of thing one look; never style unrelated things identically. |
| Common region | A shared container or background groups items regardless of spacing. | Use a card or panel when spacing alone cannot hold a group together; do not wrap everything. |
| Closure | The eye completes shapes that are only implied. | Drop unnecessary borders; let alignment and space suggest structure. |
| Continuity | The eye follows lines and alignment. | Align content on clear axes; use continuous paths for steps and timelines. |
| Figure and ground | People separate foreground from background. | Make overlays, dialogs and active layers clearly in front, with a scrim that keeps the content readable. |
| Isolation (Von Restorff) | The one thing that differs is noticed and remembered. | Make exactly one element stand out per view; several "special" elements cancel each other. |
| Aesthetic-usability effect | Polished interfaces are perceived as easier to use and forgive small problems. | Polish matters, but test with people because it can hide real usability issues. |
