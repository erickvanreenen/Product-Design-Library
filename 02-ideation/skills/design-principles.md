# Set design principles

Writes three to five principles specific enough to settle real arguments, ranked so they resolve conflicts. Optionally adds a short north-star statement for long-horizon work.

## Use when
- Decisions keep being relitigated, or a team needs shared direction.

## Steps
1. Gather inputs: research findings, product strategy, brand values, past decisions that went badly.
2. Draft candidates. For each, ask "would anyone sensible disagree?" If not, it is a platitude ("make it simple") and needs a stance ("show the total before asking for details").
3. Write each principle as: a title of three to six words, a one-line statement, the reason (linked to evidence), an example of applying it, a counter-example, and the trade-off it accepts.
4. Rank them. When two collide, the higher one wins.
5. Test against three past or current decisions. A principle that would not have changed any of them is decoration.
6. If the work is long-horizon, add a north star: one sentence on who, what experience and why it matters, with three to five pillars and a one-paragraph future scenario.

## Guardrails
- Three to five principles. More will not be remembered.
- Each principle must be testable against a design in critique.

## Output
Ranked principles in the format above, plus the optional north star.

## Done when
- Each principle has a counter-example and a trade-off, and has been tested against a real decision.
